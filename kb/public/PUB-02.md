# Slow or unstable Wi-Fi: causes and fixes

## 1. Wired fine, Wi-Fi slow: what it means
If a device connected with a network cable gets the full speed but Wi-Fi is slow or drops, the Quillnet line is
working. The cause is almost always the wireless environment in your home: distance, walls, or neighbouring
networks on the same channel.

## 2. The 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands
Quillnet routers broadcast two bands. The 2.4 GHz band reaches further but is slower and shared with many
neighbouring networks, microwaves and Bluetooth devices. The 5 GHz band is much faster and less crowded, but its
range through walls is shorter. For phones, laptops and TVs in the same or the next room, use the 5 GHz network
(its name ends in "-5G"). Keep 2.4 GHz for devices far away or for smart-home devices that only support it.

## 3. Where to put the router
Place the router high up, in the open and near the middle of your home. Keep it away from thick walls, metal
cabinets, mirrors, aquariums, microwave ovens and cordless phone bases. Do not hide it in a cupboard or behind the TV.

## 4. Crowded channels
In apartment buildings dozens of networks can share the 2.4 GHz band. Support can scan the bands remotely and see
how crowded your channel is. Switching your devices to the 5 GHz band is the quickest fix. The router chooses a
channel automatically after a restart.

## 5. Large homes
If some rooms always have weak Wi-Fi, a mesh system such as the QN-Mesh Duo covers the whole home with one network
name. Ask support about adding a mesh unit to your plan.
